Expanded Sample Project Library

Maximum Depth Edition  •  PreK–12  •  Hybrid Structure

5–10 Fully Developed Projects Per Grade Band

PreK – Grade 2

DISCOVER Stage

Exploring the world through play, curiosity, and creative choice

Structure Note: PreK–2 projects use play-based language, picture-supported steps, and very short timelines. Each project features simplified headings, oral reflection options, and heavy teacher scaffolding. AI use is teacher-led and whole-class only.

Project 1: My Favorite Thing in the World

⏱  Time Frame: 1–2 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What do I love, and how can I show others why it is so special to me?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify and name a personal interest or favorite topic
  • Create a simple visual product (drawing, model, or slideshow) that communicates ideas
  • Use descriptive words to explain their work to a peer or the class
  • Practice basic reflection using sentence stems

📚 Standards Alignment

ELA / Literacy

•  CCSS.ELA.SL.K.4 – Describe familiar people, places, things, events

•  CCSS.ELA.SL.1.5 – Add drawings or other visual displays to descriptions

•  CCSS.ELA.W.2.3 – Narrative writing with details

21st Century / SEL

•  Initiative & Self-Direction

•  Curiosity & Imagination

•  Communication

•  Creative Expression

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Day 1

Launch & Choice

• Read-aloud: a book about a child who loves something

• Class discussion: What do YOU love?

• Students pick their topic (picture menu provided)

• Begin "My Favorite Thing" cover page

Day 2

Explore & Draw

• Mini-lesson: How to add details to a drawing

• Students draw their favorite thing with labels

• Teacher conferences 1:1 with each student

Day 3

Research Moment

• Teacher reads 3–4 facts about a common topic as a model

• Students pick 2 facts to include (teacher scribes)

• Fact cards added to their project

Day 4

Create & Build

• Students choose final product: drawing, model, or poster

• Work time with materials available

• Peer share: "Look at what I made!"

Day 5

Share & Reflect

• Mini gallery walk (students place projects on desks)

• Each student says 1 sentence about their project

• Reflection: sentence stems + happy face rating

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lessons

Mini-Lesson 1: How to Choose Your Topic (Day 1, 10 minutes)

•  SAY: “Todayyou get to be the expert. An expert is someone who knows a lot about something they love.”

•  SHOW: Display the picture choice menu. Point to each category.

•  SAY: “Look at each picture. Which one makes your heart feel happy or excited?”

•  DO: Students point to or circle their choice on the menu.

•  DEBRIEF: “Notice how everyone picked something different? That’s what makes you YOU.”

Mini-Lesson 2: Adding Details to a Drawing (Day 2, 8 minutes)

•  SAY: “A great drawing tells a story without words. Details help people understand what you mean.”

•  SHOW: Draw a simple cat on the board. First: no details. Second: add stripes, whiskers, a food bowl.

•  ASK: “Which drawing tells me MORE about cats?”

•  DO: Students practice adding 2 details to their first sketch.

•  PROMPT: “What is ONE thing about your topic that most people don’t know? Draw it!”

🤖 AI Integration (Teacher-Led)

How the Teacher Uses AI

•  BEFORE the unit: Prompt — “Give me 10 simple, kid-friendly facts about [topic] for a kindergarten class.”

•  DURING the unit: Prompt — “Create a picture-based choice menu with 12 categories a 5-year-old might love (animals, sports, art, etc.).”

•  FOR differentiation: Prompt — “Simplify these 5 facts to a PreK reading level.”

•  AI is NOT used to generate student work at this stage — all creating is done by hand.

🎨 Product Options

  • Drawing with 3+ labels (PreK/K)
  • Mini poster with a title, picture, and 2 facts (Grade 1)
  • Simple 3-slide presentation: My topic / Why I love it / One cool fact (Grade 2)
  • LEGO or block model with a verbal explanation
  • Show-and-tell video (60 seconds, teacher-recorded)

📋 Checkpoints

  • Checkpoint 1 — Day 1: Topic chosen (teacher records on class tracker)
  • Checkpoint 2 — Day 2: Drawing started with at least 1 detail
  • Checkpoint 3 — Day 4: Product mostly complete (teacher gives verbal feedback)
  • Checkpoint 4 — Day 5: Student shares with at least one peer

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Picture choice menus instead of written options

•  Sentence stems on cards: “I love ___ because ___.”

•  Teacher scribes facts for non-writers

•  Partner system: buddy helps glue/label

•  Simplified 1-step directions on task card

Extensions

•  Write a short nonfiction paragraph

•  Create a ‘Top 5 Facts’ list independently

•  Interview a family member about the same topic

•  Make a second project on a connected topic

•  Teach a younger student about their topic

💡 Reflection Prompts

  • PreK: “Point to your favorite part. Tell me one word about it.”
  • K/Grade 1: “I chose ___ because ___. My favorite part was ___.”
  • Grade 2: “I learned ___ about my topic. I am proud of ___ because ___. Next time I would ___.”

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Photo of final product
  • Completed reflection card or drawing
  • Teacher observation note
  • Video of student share (optional)

Project 2: Build a Better Playground

⏱  Time Frame: 2–3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What makes a playground fun, safe, and good for everyone?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Apply simple design thinking: observe a problem, generate ideas, build a solution
  • Use shapes, measurement language, and spatial reasoning in a real context
  • Collaborate with a partner or small group to build and explain a design
  • Present a model verbally using simple descriptive language

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Notice & Wonder

• Go outside and observe the current playground

• Students draw what they notice (good things + problems)

• Class brainstorm: What would make recess even better?

Days 3–4

Research & Plan

• Teacher shares 3–4 images of creative playgrounds

• Mini-lesson: What makes something safe AND fun?

• Students sketch their design idea with labels

Days 5–6

Build It

• Students build using blocks, LEGO, cardboard, clay

• Partner feedback: “What do you like? What would you add?”

• Students revise based on feedback

Day 7

Present & Reflect

• Mini gallery walk of all designs

• Each student explains their design to a small group

• Reflection using sentence stems

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: What Makes Something Safe AND Fun?

Day 3 — 15 minutes

•  SAY: “Designers think about two things at once: Is it FUN? And is it SAFE?”

•  SHOW: Two playground images — one clearly dangerous (broken equipment), one safe and creative.

•  DISCUSS: “Which one would you rather play on? Why?”

•  TEACH: Introduce 3 simple design rules: (1) No sharp edges, (2) Soft landing zones, (3) Space for everyone.

•  DO: Students look at their own sketch and circle anything that might need to be safer.

•  DEBRIEF: “Great designers change their ideas when they learn something new. That’s called revision!”

🤖 AI Integration

Teacher Prompts for This Project

•  “Generate 8 images of creative and accessible playground equipment for a PreK–2 classroom discussion.”

•  “Create a simple T-chart graphic for kindergarteners: Safe vs. Not Safe on a playground.”

•  “Give me 6 kid-friendly engineering challenges using playground themes (ramps, swings, climbing walls).”

🎨 Product Options

  • Block or LEGO model with labeled diagram
  • Crayon blueprint (top-down sketch with labels)
  • Clay sculpture model
  • Digital drawing (iPad) with voice-recorded explanation

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-labeled diagram template

•  Shape sorting cards for design ideas

•  Partner builds together, sharing roles

•  Teacher photographs each step as documentation

Extensions

•  Calculate how many kids could use it at once

•  Write 3 safety rules for their design

•  Create a brochure for the ‘new playground’

•  Redesign for a student with a physical disability

💡 Reflection Prompts

  • PreK: “Point to your favorite part of your playground.”
  • K/1: “I built ___ because ___. I changed my design when I learned ___.”
  • Grade 2: “My design helps kids by ___. If I could build it for real, I would also add ___.”

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Photo of model + sketch blueprint
  • Reflection sentence / card
  • Peer feedback card

Project 3: Community Helpers We Count On

⏱  Time Frame: 2 weeks

❓  Driving Question: Who helps our community and how can we say thank you?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify community helpers and describe their roles
  • Ask simple interview-style questions and record answers with a partner
  • Create a thank-you product that is meaningful and audience-aware
  • Practice speaking and listening skills in a real context

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Who Helps Us?

• Read-aloud about community helpers

• Class anchor chart: who helps our school/neighborhood

• Students pick a helper they admire

Days 3–4

Interview Prep

• Mini-lesson: How to ask a good question

• Practice with a partner: “What is your favorite part of your job?”

• Teacher arranges a brief visit or video from a local helper

Days 5–6

Research & Create

• Students draw, write, or record 2–3 facts about their helper

• Begin thank-you product: card, video, poster, or puppet show

Day 7–8

Present & Deliver

• Share products with class

• Deliver thank-you cards to actual helpers (school nurse, custodian, etc.)

• Reflection and portfolio capture

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: How to Ask a Good Question

Day 3 — 10 minutes

•  SAY: “An interview is when you ask someone questions to learn from them.”

•  SHOW: Display two questions on the board: “Do you like your job?” (yes/no) vs. “What is the best part of your job?” (open-ended).

•  DISCUSS: “Which question helps us learn more?”

•  TEACH: Questions that start with WHAT, HOW, WHY, TELL ME ABOUT teach us more than yes/no questions.

•  DO: Partners practice asking each other one open question and listening carefully.

•  DEBRIEF: Collect student questions to use in the real interview.

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Picture cards of community helpers

•  Question sentence starters on cards

•  Scribing support for non-writers

•  Option to draw instead of write

Extensions

•  Write a 2-paragraph biography

•  Design a ‘help wanted’ poster for a community helper job

•  Research a helper in another country

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Thank-you product (card, video, poster)
  • 1–2 interview questions they created
  • Reflection drawing or sentence

Project 4: The Weather Scientists

⏱  Time Frame: 2–3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: How does weather affect what we do every day, and how do scientists study it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Observe, record, and describe daily weather patterns
  • Use simple science tools (thermometer pictures, rain gauges, wind vanes) in play contexts
  • Connect weather to personal choices (clothing, activities, safety)
  • Create a weather report or weather journal as a final product

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

What is Weather?

• Watch a short weather video

• Class anchor chart: rain, snow, sunny, windy, cloudy, stormy

• Students draw their favorite weather and share why

Days 3–6

Weather Scientists

• Daily weather observation & class chart

• Mini-lessons: clouds, temperature, wind

• Students keep a personal weather journal (draw + label)

Days 7–8

Create & Report

• Students choose a product: weather report video, poster, or journal

• Practice presenting weather like a TV meteorologist

• Peer rehearsal

Day 9–10

Share & Reflect

• Present weather reports to class or record videos

• Reflection

• Portfolio capture

🤖 AI Integration

Teacher Prompts

•  “Create a 10-day weather observation chart template for kindergarteners with picture boxes and simple labels.”

•  “Write a short, simple script a kindergartener could read as a TV weather reporter.”

•  “Give me 5 fun weather experiments I can do in a PreK classroom with no special equipment.”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-labeled weather journal pages

•  Weather word wall with pictures

•  Video clips instead of text-based research

•  Teacher-recorded weather data for reference

Extensions

•  Track weather for 2 full weeks and spot a pattern

•  Research a famous storm or weather event

•  Create a forecast for a week using real weather data

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Weather journal (10-day collection)
  • Weather report video or poster
  • Reflection drawing/sentence

Project 5: Story Makers: Create Your Own Picture Book

⏱  Time Frame: 3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What story do I want to tell, and how can I make it come alive for readers?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Understand the structure of a story: beginning, middle, end
  • Create original characters and a simple plot
  • Illustrate a multi-page book with matching words and pictures
  • Read or present the book to an authentic audience (class, library, families)

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Story Exploration

• Study 3 mentor picture books: what makes them work?

• Class chart: characters, problem, solution

• Students brainstorm story ideas using an idea web

Days 3–4

Plan Your Story

• Mini-lesson: Story mountain (beginning, middle, end)

• Students create a 3-box story plan

• Teacher conferences with each student

Days 5–8

Write & Illustrate

• Daily writing + illustrating work time

• Peer share: “Read” your book to a partner

• Revise one thing based on feedback

Days 9–10

Polish & Publish

• Add a title, author name, and dedication page

• Cover design

• Practice reading aloud

Days 11–12

Author Celebration

• Book share with class, another class, or families

• Display books in the classroom library

• Reflection

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: Story Mountain

Day 3 — 12 minutes

•  DRAW a mountain on the board with 5 points: Beginning, Problem, Exciting Part, Solution, Ending.

•  SAY: “Every great story goes on a journey. It starts small, then something happens — that’s the problem — then it gets solved!”

•  SHOW: Use a familiar book (e.g., The Very Hungry Caterpillar) and label each part on the mountain.

•  DO: Students draw their own mountain and fill in 3 boxes: Who is in my story? What is the problem? How does it end?

•  TEACHER TIP: Some students will need you to scribe. That’s fine! Their ideas are the most important part.

🤖 AI Integration

Teacher Prompts

•  “Generate 10 simple story starter ideas appropriate for kindergarteners (e.g., ‘One day, a tiny dragon found a missing sock’).”

•  “Create a picture book page template with a large illustration box and 2-line writing area.”

•  “Give me a list of descriptive words a 1st grader could use for a story about animals.”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-made 3-box story planning sheet

•  Word wall for story vocabulary

•  Sentence starters: “Once there was...” “Then one day...” “Finally...”

•  Option for wordless picture book (illustrations only)

Extensions

•  Write 8+ pages with richer vocabulary

•  Add dialogue between characters

•  Create a second book in a series

•  Record an audio version of the book

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Completed picture book
  • Story planning sheet
  • Read-aloud video (optional)
  • Reflection: “My story is about ___ and I am proud of ___ because ___.”

Project 6: Animals We Love: Research & Create

⏱  Time Frame: 2–3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What is the most interesting animal in the world, and what can I teach others about it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Select an animal of personal interest and gather basic facts from simple sources
  • Organize information into a simple research product (drawing, poster, or booklet)
  • Share learning with an audience using descriptive vocabulary
  • Practice citing a source (“I learned this from ___”)

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Pick Your Animal

• Animal picture gallery walk — students choose their animal

• Watch a short video about their chosen animal

• “I Wonder” journal entry: 2 questions they want to learn

Days 3–4

Research Time

• Teacher provides 2–3 simple fact sheets or picture books per animal

• Students find and circle 5 key facts

• Draw the animal in its habitat

Days 5–6

Create & Organize

• Mini-lesson: How to organize a poster

• Students build their product (poster, booklet, slides)

• Add pictures + labels + facts

Day 7

Present & Celebrate

• Animal museum walk — students stand by their project and explain

• Peer questions: “What surprised you most?”

• Reflection

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Fact sheet templates pre-labeled with animals

•  Teacher reads sources aloud; students highlight

•  5-fact graphic organizer with picture boxes

Extensions

•  Compare two animals using a Venn diagram

•  Write a fictional story featuring their animal

•  Research an endangered animal and propose how to help

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Animal research product (poster, booklet, or slides)
  • I Wonder questions card
  • Reflection sentence

Project 7: Inventor's Workshop: Fix a Problem!

⏱  Time Frame: 2 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What is a problem I notice every day, and what could I invent to fix it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Apply design thinking at a beginner level: identify problem, brainstorm, create, share
  • Use materials creatively to prototype a simple invention
  • Explain what their invention does and why it would help people
  • Give and receive simple feedback with kind and specific language

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Spot the Problem

• Read: What Do You Do With a Problem? (Kobi Yamada)

• Class brainstorm: problems we notice at school or home

• Students choose 1 problem they want to solve

Days 3–4

Brainstorm Solutions

• Mini-lesson: “Inventors come up with MANY ideas before picking one”

• Students draw 3 possible inventions on an idea sheet

• Choose the best idea and explain why

Days 5–7

Build the Prototype

• Students build with recyclables, blocks, clay, paper

• Teacher circulates and asks: “How does it work? What problem does it solve?”

• Add a name and a label to the invention

Days 8–9

Present & Improve

• Invention convention: each student presents their invention

• Peer feedback: “I like ___ because ___. I wonder if ___...”

• Optional: revise based on feedback

Day 10

Reflect

• Class reflection circle

• Portfolio capture

• “If my invention was real, it would help people by ___.”

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: Inventors Have LOTS of Ideas First

Day 3 — 10 minutes

•  SAY: “The first idea an inventor has is almost never the final one. Inventors fill pages with ideas — then pick the best one.”

•  SHOW: Share a real-life example (e.g., the inventor of the Post-it Note made a bad glue first — then turned it into something useful!).

•  DO: Students get a sheet with 3 empty boxes. They must draw a different invention idea in each box.

•  SHARE: Partners explain each idea in one sentence.

•  CHOOSE: Students circle their favorite and write or dictate WHY it’s the best idea.

•  TEACHER TIP: Praise unusual ideas! “Wow, that’s really creative thinking” builds confidence.

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Brainstorm template with 3 boxes + pictures

•  Problem picture cards (messy backpack, wet shoes, loud noises)

•  Partner builds together with shared materials

•  Simplified feedback: thumbs up + one question

Extensions

•  Write a TV commercial for their invention

•  Calculate the cost of materials their invention would need

•  Design a logo and brand name for their product

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Photo of invention model
  • Idea brainstorm sheet (3-box planning)
  • Labeled diagram
  • Reflection: “My invention helps people by ___.”
