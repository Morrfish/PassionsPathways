How this trainer works, and why

It borrows the parts that the best-known programs and the adult training research agree on.

  • Start small, add one at a time. You begin with three notes spread far apart. When you score 90% over your last 30 answers, the app offers the next note. This is the core of the Wong lab's 2025 online study, where adult musicians reached 90% accuracy on an average of seven pitch classes after about 21 hours over eight weeks.
  • Learn the note, not the frequency. Each question picks a random octave and, on Mixed, a random instrument. That 2025 study trained pitch class this way, and the skill carried over to an instrument people had not trained on.
  • No reference tones. Relative pitch is useful but it is not what you are training here. The ear-clearing scatter stops you comparing each note with the one before.
  • Chords first, the Eguchi way. The Eguchi method (the basis of the Chordy and Tonal Ear Trainer apps) teaches children chords as whole sounds with a color flag each, starting from C, F and G major and adding chords one at a time. The Chord names drill follows the same order.
  • Colors as a memory hook. Like the Synesthesia app, each pitch class has a fixed color across the whole app.
  • More practice on your weak spots. Notes you miss come up more often, and the Stats tab shows which pairs you mix up.

Results from adult training vary a lot. In the published studies roughly one in five to one in three committed adults reached a strict absolute pitch standard, and many more improved a lot. Daily practice and patience over months matter more than long sessions.
