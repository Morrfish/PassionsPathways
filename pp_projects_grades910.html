Grades 9–10

DEEPEN Stage

Connecting passions to real-world problems, academic rigor, and emerging futures

Structure Note: Grades 9–10 projects use formal proposal writing, annotated bibliographies, community or career connections, and comprehensive AI logs with ethical reflection. Products are professional quality. Presentations include Q&A with adults or community members.

Project 1: Passion-Driven Research Documentary

⏱  Time Frame: 6–7 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What topic do I care about deeply enough to investigate rigorously, and how can I use documentary film to present evidence-based findings in a way that moves an audience to think or act?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Conduct academic research using primary and secondary sources including at least one original interview
  • Construct and defend a clear thesis or central argument through the documentary form
  • Write, storyboard, film, edit, and screen a 6–10 minute documentary film
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of documentary choices (narration, music, pacing, evidence) through audience feedback

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–3

Topic, Thesis & Research Foundation

• Choose topic connected to a genuine passion or current issue

• Write a working thesis: a clear, arguable central claim (not just a topic)

• Conduct background research: 3–5 credible sources; identify knowledge gaps

Days 4–6

Advanced Research

• Add 2–3 additional sources including primary data, academic articles, or government reports

• Conduct 1–2 formal interviews (expert, community member, or affected individual)

• Build annotated bibliography (6–8 sources minimum)

Days 7–10

Script & Storyboard

• Write full narration script with thesis woven throughout

• Identify which evidence appears visually vs. narrated vs. interview

• Storyboard every scene: location, camera angle, narration or interview line

Days 11–15

Film & Edit

• Film narration, B-roll, and interviews

• Edit: pacing, music, titles, color grading

• Peer screening with structured feedback form

Days 16–18

Premiere & Debrief

• Film premiere with invited guests (teachers, parents, community)

• Director Q&A after screening

• Reflection essay and portfolio

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: Building a Thesis-Driven Documentary

Day 2 — 25 minutes

•  SAY: “Most student documentaries fall into a trap: they report facts instead of making an argument. The most powerful documentaries don’t just show — they claim.”

•  SHOW two thesis examples:

•     REPORT (weak): “My documentary is about climate change.”

•     CLAIM (strong): “This documentary argues that local farming communities are disproportionately harmed by climate policy designed to protect urban areas.”

•  DISCUSS: What is the CLAIM in the second example? Who might disagree? Why does disagreement make it stronger?

•  TEACH: A strong documentary thesis is: specific, arguable, evidence-based, and connected to real people’s lives.

•  DRAFT: Students write 3 possible thesis statements for their topic.

•  WORKSHOP: In pairs, identify the most arguable thesis — the one a reasonable person could push back on.

•  DEBRIEF: “Every scene in your documentary should serve your thesis. If a scene doesn’t, cut it.”

🤖 AI Integration (Advanced & Ethical)

Approved Uses — All Must Be Logged and Verified

•  Research scan: “List 5 credible academic or government sources about [topic]. Include authors and publication names.” Students look up and verify each source independently.

•  Argument stress test: “Here is my thesis. What are the 3 strongest counterarguments? How might a critic attack it?” Students revise to address the strongest critique.

•  Script clarity: “Read this narration passage. Is the argument clear? What might confuse an audience unfamiliar with this topic?”

•  Interview prep: “I’m interviewing [expert role] about [topic]. What are 5 questions that would get them to reveal something surprising or personal?”

•  ETHICAL NOTE: Students must include an AI Ethics Reflection in their final portfolio: how AI was used, how they verified its outputs, and what they deliberately chose to do themselves.

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Annotated bibliography template with sentence frames

•  Storyboard with 12 pre-labeled boxes

•  Interview question stems by topic type

•  Shorter format: 4–6 minutes

•  Co-director option with divided responsibilities

Extensions

•  Submit to a student film festival (regional or national)

•  Add original score or sound design

•  Write a companion academic essay analyzing documentary choices

•  Present findings to a policy-relevant audience

💡 Reflection Prompts

  • How did your thesis change from your initial idea to the final film? What evidence changed your thinking?
  • What was the most difficult creative or research decision you made, and why?
  • How do you know whether your documentary achieved its intended effect on the audience?
  • What ethical responsibility do documentary filmmakers have, and how did you honor that in your work?
  • Where did AI support your process, and where did human judgment matter more?

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Annotated bibliography (6–8 sources)
  • Final script + storyboard
  • Completed documentary (file + screening link)
  • Peer feedback form from screening
  • AI log + Ethics Reflection
  • Director’s reflection essay

Project 2: Design a Solution to a Local Problem

⏱  Time Frame: 6–7 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What real problem exists in my local community, and how can I design, prototype, and pitch a research-backed solution to people who have the power to implement it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify a specific, local problem through original research and community engagement
  • Apply design thinking and systems analysis to develop a feasible solution
  • Build and test a prototype or detailed plan with real community stakeholders
  • Present findings and recommendations in a professional pitch format to decision-makers

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–3

Community Research

• Identify 3 potential community problems

• Conduct empathy interviews with 4–5 community members

• Analyze data and select highest-priority problem

Days 4–6

Systems Analysis

• Map the root causes and contributing factors using a systems diagram

• Research how other cities/towns have addressed similar problems

• Identify constraints: budget, political, social, logistical

Days 7–10

Design & Prototype

• Generate 10+ solution ideas; filter using criteria matrix

• Develop top solution into a detailed proposal or physical prototype

• Present prototype to 3 real stakeholders; collect structured feedback

Days 11–13

Revise & Pitch Prep

• Revise solution based on stakeholder feedback

• Build professional pitch deck (12–15 slides)

• Practice with teacher and peers; run full Q&A rehearsal

Days 14–16

Pitch to Decision-Makers

• Present to city official, nonprofit director, school board, or business leader

• Respond to challenging questions

• Reflection + impact documentation

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: Systems Thinking — Finding Root Causes

Day 4 — 25 minutes

•  SAY: “Every visible problem is just the surface. Underneath is a system of causes feeding it. Good designers fix the root, not just the symptom.”

•  EXAMPLE: Problem: Students are frequently absent. Surface fix: Call parents. Root causes: housing instability, transportation, bullying, mental health, unsafe neighborhood.

•  TEACH: The 5 Whys technique. Ask “Why?” five times in a row to drill to the root.

•  DEMO: “Why do students miss school? → Why are they afraid to come? → Why is the environment threatening? → Why aren’t those situations addressed? → Why does the school lack those resources?”

•  DO: Students take their chosen problem and use the 5 Whys to trace it to root causes.

•  DISCUSS: “Which root cause is most actionable? Which one, if solved, would create the most change?”

•  DEBRIEF: “Your solution should target the root cause, not the symptom. That’s what separates an effective intervention from a temporary fix.”

🤖 AI Integration

Approved Uses

•  Systems mapping: “What are the systemic causes of [problem] in communities like mine? Provide 5 factors with brief explanations.” Students validate against research.

•  Solution comparison: “Describe how 3 different cities have addressed [problem]. What were the outcomes?” Students find original sources to verify.

•  Pitch feedback: “Here is my pitch deck outline. What questions is a city official most likely to ask? What might they find unconvincing?”

•  Criteria matrix: “Help me build an 8-factor evaluation matrix to compare my top 3 solutions.” Students assign their own scores.

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Interview question bank (community-focused)

•  Systems diagram template

•  Simplified pitch deck (8 slides)

•  Co-designer partner option

•  Teacher-guided stakeholder identification

Extensions

•  Submit formal written proposal to local government

•  Partner with a community organization to begin implementation

•  Create a data visualization of community problem

•  Apply for a youth grant to fund the solution

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Empathy interview transcripts or notes
  • Systems diagram
  • Prototype or detailed solution plan
  • Stakeholder feedback documentation
  • Professional pitch deck
  • AI log + Ethics Reflection
  • Reflection essay

Project 3: Passion-Based Business or Social Enterprise Plan

⏱  Time Frame: 6–7 weeks

❓  Driving Question: How can I turn a skill or passion I have into a viable business or social enterprise that creates real value for a specific audience?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Research a market, customer base, or social need relevant to their passion
  • Apply entrepreneurship frameworks (business model canvas, value proposition, SWOT analysis)
  • Create a comprehensive business or social enterprise plan with financial projections
  • Pitch to a panel of evaluators (including at least one community or business professional)

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–3

Opportunity Identification

• Map passion → skill → value for others

• Research: Who else offers something similar? What gap exists?

• Conduct 5+ customer discovery interviews: “What problem do you face with X?”

Days 4–6

Business Model Design

• Complete a Business Model Canvas (9 blocks)

• Define: value proposition, customer segments, revenue streams, channels, key activities

• Conduct basic market sizing research

Days 7–10

Financial Planning & Prototype

• Estimate startup costs, revenue, and break-even timeline

• Build a minimum viable product or prototype service

• Test with 3 potential customers; collect feedback

Days 11–13

Full Plan & Pitch Prep

• Write complete business plan (executive summary, market analysis, financials, operations)

• Build professional pitch deck

• Practice pitch with full Q&A

Days 14–16

Pitch to Panel

• Present to panel including entrepreneur, teacher, and community member

• Respond to investment-style questions

• Reflection + next steps plan

🤖 AI Integration

Approved Uses

•  Market research: “What are the demographics, habits, and spending patterns of [customer segment]?” Students verify with 2 sources.

•  SWOT analysis: “Based on my business idea [description], what might be the 3 biggest threats I should plan for?”

•  Financial modeling: “If I sell [product] at [$X] and my monthly costs are [$Y], how long until I break even if I sell [Z] units per month?”

•  Pitch feedback: “Read my executive summary. Does it make an investor want to know more? What’s missing?”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-filled Business Model Canvas template

•  Customer interview question bank

•  Simplified financial template (basic revenue/cost)

•  Social enterprise option (nonprofit focus rather than profit)

•  Partner business team option

Extensions

•  Launch the business (even at small scale)

•  Apply to a youth entrepreneurship competition

•  Build a real website or Etsy shop

•  Create a full investor memorandum

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Customer discovery interview notes
  • Business Model Canvas
  • Financial projections
  • Business plan document
  • Pitch deck
  • AI log + Ethics Reflection
  • Reflection: What did I learn about entrepreneurship and my own passion?

Project 4: Passion Meets Policy: An Advocacy & Research Project

⏱  Time Frame: 6 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What policy issue intersects with my passion, and how can I research it deeply enough to argue for a specific change using evidence, data, and ethical reasoning?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Research a policy issue from multiple perspectives using academic and primary sources
  • Construct and defend a specific, evidence-based policy recommendation
  • Understand how advocacy works and what makes it effective or ineffective
  • Present a formal position paper and verbal testimony to a relevant audience

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–3

Issue & Background Research

• Select a policy area connected to passion (environment, health, education, housing, arts, technology)

• Research historical background of the issue

• Identify current law/policy and key stakeholders

Days 4–6

Multiple Perspectives

• Research 3 different positions on the issue

• Identify the strongest arguments on each side

• Map where the real disagreement lies (values? facts? priorities?)

Days 7–9

Develop Position

• Draft specific policy recommendation

• Gather evidence: data, case studies, expert opinion, ethics

• Anticipate and address counterarguments

Days 10–12

Position Paper

• Write formal position paper (4–6 pages)

• Structure: introduction, background, multiple perspectives, argument, counterarguments, conclusion

• Peer review and revision

Days 13–15

Testimony & Presentation

• Write 3-minute testimony (as if presenting to a committee)

• Present to class + invited community member or official

• Reflection on advocacy, evidence, and civic responsibility

🤖 AI Integration

Approved Uses

•  Multiple perspectives: “Explain the strongest arguments FOR and AGAINST [policy]. Identify where each side prioritizes different values.” Students evaluate and cite original sources for each argument.

•  Counterargument check: “Read my policy argument. What are the 2 strongest objections someone opposed to my position would raise?” Students address each in their paper.

•  Evidence scan: “What data or research supports [claim] in the context of [policy area]?” Students locate original studies.

•  Ethics: “What ethical frameworks (utilitarian, rights-based, justice-focused) apply to this policy issue? How do they lead to different conclusions?”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-mapped policy issue list with background summaries

•  Position paper template with section headers

•  Testimony sentence starters

•  Shortened paper: 2–3 pages with teacher support

Extensions

•  Submit testimony to a real government body or agency

•  Create a policy brief for a nonprofit or advocacy organization

•  Research and compare how other countries address the same issue

•  Turn the position paper into an op-ed for a real publication

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Research notes (multiple perspectives organized)
  • Position paper
  • Testimony script
  • Presentation recording
  • AI log + Ethics Reflection
  • Reflection: How did researching multiple perspectives change or strengthen your position?

Project 5: Passion Portfolio: Building Your Future

⏱  Time Frame: 6–8 weeks

❓  Driving Question: How can I strategically use a passion-based project to build real skills, explore career pathways, and create a portfolio artifact that represents who I am becoming?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify connections between a current passion and realistic future pathways (career, college program, trade, entrepreneurship, service)
  • Complete a substantial, independently-managed project in a chosen domain
  • Build a professional-quality digital portfolio artifact to represent the project
  • Reflect on personal growth, skills developed, and how the passion shapes future direction

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–3

Pathway Mapping

• Passion inventory: strengths, interests, experiences

• Map passion → skill → possible career/pathway connections

• Research 2–3 specific pathways and what they require

Days 4–6

Project Design

• Choose a project that builds skills for their chosen pathway

• Write a formal project proposal (purpose, product, timeline, standards)

• Identify a mentor or advisor (teacher, community member, or online expert)

Days 7–14

Project Execution

• Execute project with weekly check-ins

• Mentor consultation at least twice

• Document process: photos, notes, drafts, reflections

Days 15–16

Portfolio Building

• Design professional digital portfolio page for this project

• Include: overview, process documentation, final product, reflection, skills demonstrated

• Peer review of portfolio page

Days 17–18

Presentation

• Student-led conference format: walk teacher/advisor through portfolio

• Discuss: How does this connect to my future? What did I discover?

• Final reflection

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pathway mapping template with career clusters

•  Weekly check-in form with sentence frames

•  Portfolio page template

•  Co-project with a partner

Extensions

•  Complete a micro-internship or job shadow connected to pathway

•  Create 3+ portfolio pages representing different skill areas

•  Submit portfolio for review to a college admissions advisor or HR professional

•  Use project as basis for a college application essay or interview response

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Pathway mapping document
  • Formal project proposal
  • Mentor consultation notes
  • Process documentation (photos, drafts, notes)
  • Final project artifact
  • Professional portfolio page
  • AI log + Ethics Reflection
  • Reflection: Who am I becoming, and how does this passion point toward my future?
