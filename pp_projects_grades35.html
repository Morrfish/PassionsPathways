Grades 3–5

EXPLORE Stage

Turning curiosity into research, products, and authentic sharing

Structure Note: Grades 3–5 projects use structured inquiry sections, I Wonder questions, and organized note-taking. Products move from purely creative to research-supported. AI use is whole-class or small-group with teacher facilitation. Reflection is written in 2–4 sentences.

Project 1: Genius Hour: Follow Your Curiosity

⏱  Time Frame: 3–4 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What topic excites me most, and what can I create to teach others about it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Generate researchable “I Wonder” questions from a personal interest
  • Use 2–3 credible sources to gather and organize information
  • Create a product of choice that demonstrates content knowledge and creativity
  • Present learning to an authentic audience using clear, organized language

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Passion Discovery

• Interest inventory: 20 categories with checkboxes

• Students rank top 3 interests

• Generate 3–5 “I Wonder” questions using sentence stems

Days 3–4

Choose & Plan

• Select one driving question

• Mini-lesson: What makes a good research question?

• Draft a simple project plan (topic, question, product idea, timeline)

Days 5–8

Research Phase

• Explore 2–3 sources: books, kid-safe websites, videos

• Take notes using boxes-and-bullets organizer

• Check: Are my notes in MY own words?

Days 9–12

Create Phase

• Mini-lessons on product craft (design, speaking, writing)

• Daily work time with teacher conferences

• Peer draft share and feedback

Days 13–14

Share & Reflect

• Genius Hour showcase (gallery walk or presentations)

• Reflection journal entry

• Portfolio upload or binder addition

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: What Makes a Good Research Question?

Day 3 — 15 minutes

•  SAY: “Your driving question is the HEART of your whole project. If it’s too small, your project will be boring. If it’s too big, you’ll get lost.”

•  SHOW three examples on the board:

•     TOO SMALL: “What color is a flamingo?” (one fact answers it)

•     TOO BIG: “How does the entire ocean work?” (you could write a textbook!)

•     JUST RIGHT: “Why do flamingos stand on one leg and what does it tell us about animal behavior?”

•  PRACTICE: Each student takes their interest and writes 3 possible questions. Partners rate them: too small / too big / just right.

•  SELECT: Students circle their best question and share with the class.

•  DEBRIEF: “The best research questions make YOU want to find the answer — and they make your reader curious too.”

🤖 AI Integration

Student-Facing (Whole-Class or Small Group with Teacher)

•  BRAINSTORM: “Ask AI to give you 10 sub-questions about your topic. Pick 2 that interest you most.”

•  VOCABULARY: “Use AI to define 3 tricky words from your research in simpler language.”

•  FEEDBACK: “Paste your draft paragraph into AI and ask: What is confusing? How could I improve my introduction?”

•  NOT ALLOWED: Using AI to write the project, answer research questions without verification, or skip reading sources.

AI Log Template (Required)

•  What tool did I use?

•  What did I ask it?

•  How did I use the response?

•  Did I check if the information was accurate? How?

•  What did I still have to do myself?

🎨 Product Options

  • Poster with at least 3 sections and visuals
  • 3–6 slide presentation with speaker notes
  • Short video (2–4 minutes) with script and visuals
  • Diorama or physical model with labeled diagram + explanation card
  • Illustrated mini-book or comic strip (4–8 pages)
  • Podcast episode with intro, facts, and conclusion

📋 Checkpoints & Conferences

  • Checkpoint 1 — Day 2: Driving question + product choice approved by teacher
  • Checkpoint 2 — Day 6: Research notes complete (teacher reviews note-taking quality)
  • Checkpoint 3 — Day 10: Draft product shared with peer; feedback given
  • Checkpoint 4 — Day 13: Final product complete, reflection written

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-made graphic organizer with note-taking boxes

•  Sentence stems for reflection and presentation

•  Teacher reads sources aloud; students highlight

•  Simplified product options (drawing + 5 facts)

•  Chunked timeline with daily task cards

Extensions

•  Use 4–5 sources including at least one interview

•  Create two different products on the same topic

•  Write a bibliography in basic citation format

•  Present to a younger class or at a school event

•  Submit project to a school newsletter or display case

💡 Reflection Prompts

  • 3rd: “I learned ___ about my topic. Something surprising was ___. Next time I would ___.”
  • 4th: “My driving question was ___ and I found out ___. The hardest part was ___ because ___.”
  • 5th: “My research changed my thinking about ___. If I continued this project, I would ___. AI helped me by ___ but I had to ___ myself.”

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Completed interest inventory
  • Research notes (boxes-and-bullets organizer)
  • Final product (photo, video, or file)
  • AI log
  • Reflection journal entry

Project 2: Invention Convention

⏱  Time Frame: 3–4 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What problem do I notice in my school, home, or community, and what could I invent to solve it?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Apply a 5-step design process: identify problem → research → brainstorm → build → reflect
  • Write and present an inventor’s pitch explaining the problem, solution, and impact
  • Use basic research to support design decisions with evidence
  • Revise a prototype based on peer testing and feedback

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Problem Identification

• Observe surroundings: school, home, neighborhood

• List 5 problems they notice

• Choose 1 problem to solve and write a problem statement

Days 3–4

Research Phase

• Research: Has anyone tried to solve this before?

• Find 1–2 existing products/solutions

• Note: What works? What is missing?

Days 5–6

Brainstorm

• Sketch 3 different invention ideas

• Test criteria: Will it solve the problem? Is it buildable? Is it safe?

• Choose best idea and complete a planning sheet

Days 7–9

Build Prototype

• Build using available materials

• Peer test: Does it work?

• Record what worked and what didn’t

Days 10–12

Pitch & Present

• Inventor’s pitch (2–3 min): problem, solution, demo, impact

• Q&A from class

• Reflection and portfolio capture

🏫 Scripted Mini-Lesson: Writing a Problem Statement

Day 2 — 15 minutes

•  SAY: “Every great invention starts with a great problem. A problem statement tells us: WHO is affected, WHAT the problem is, and WHY it matters.”

•  SHOW a model: “Students at our school often forget their lunch money. This causes them to miss lunch or borrow from others. A wristband reminder system could help.”

•  BREAK IT DOWN: Who? Students. What? Forget lunch money. Why it matters? Miss meals.

•  DO: Students draft their own problem statement using a 3-part template: [WHO] often [WHAT PROBLEM]. This causes [WHY IT MATTERS].

•  SHARE: 3–4 students read theirs aloud. Peers give thumbs up or suggest improvements.

•  DEBRIEF: “The more specific and real your problem is, the more powerful your invention will be.”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-made problem statement template

•  Picture list of common school/home problems

•  Partner collaboration on invention

•  Simplified build: draw the invention and explain how it works

Extensions

•  Write a formal proposal to a school leader

•  Create a commercial or advertisement for the invention

•  Research the manufacturing cost

•  Improve the prototype based on 3 rounds of testing

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Problem statement
  • Research notes (existing solutions)
  • 3-idea brainstorm sketch
  • Photo of prototype
  • Inventor’s pitch notes or recording
  • Reflection

Project 3: Create a Museum Exhibit

⏱  Time Frame: 3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: How can I turn my knowledge into an experience that teaches and inspires others?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Conduct multi-source research and synthesize information into display form
  • Design an engaging exhibit with text, visuals, and interactive elements
  • Present as an “expert docent” to visitors (classmates, other students, families)
  • Use audience feedback to reflect on communication effectiveness

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Topic & Research Questions

• Choose exhibit topic (science, history, art, culture, animals, technology)

• Write 3–5 research questions

• Preview 2–3 sources and begin notes

Days 3–5

Research Phase

• Gather information from books, websites, and videos

• Organize notes into sections: Who/What, How, Why It Matters, Fun Facts

• Create a source list

Days 6–8

Design the Exhibit

• Plan exhibit layout (title, panels, visuals, interactive element)

• Mini-lesson: What makes a great museum display?

• Assemble exhibit with clear labels and engaging design

Days 9–10

Docent Practice

• Write a 1–2 minute docent script

• Practice with a partner

• Prepare for 2–3 questions visitors might ask

Days 11–12

Museum Day & Reflect

• Exhibit open house (rotating visits from other classes or families)

• Collect visitor feedback cards

• Reflection and portfolio capture

🤖 AI Integration

Student Prompts (Supervised)

•  “Ask AI: Give me 5 interesting facts about [topic] that most people don’t know.” Then verify each fact using a second source.

•  “Ask AI: What would be a good interactive activity for a museum exhibit about [topic]?” Students evaluate ideas and choose one.

•  “Ask AI: What questions might a visitor ask about [topic]? Help me prepare 3 answers.”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Exhibit planning template with labeled sections

•  Sentence stems for docent script

•  Pre-found sources for struggling researchers

•  Partner exhibit instead of solo

Extensions

•  Add an audio component (QR code linking to recording)

•  Create a companion brochure

•  Submit a summary article to the school newsletter

•  Design a virtual museum page using Google Slides

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Research notes (organized by section)
  • Photo of exhibit
  • Docent script
  • Visitor feedback cards
  • Reflection

Project 4: Passion Portrait: A Person Who Inspires Me

⏱  Time Frame: 2–3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: Who has pursued their passion and changed the world, and what can I learn from their journey?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Research a real person using multiple sources and take organized notes
  • Connect the subject’s passion journey to students’ own interests and goals
  • Create a biography-based product that tells a compelling story
  • Write or present an analysis of how passion led to impact

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Choose & Research

• Brainstorm people connected to student interests (athlete, artist, scientist, inventor, activist)

• Choose a subject and generate 3–5 research questions

• Begin research from 2 sources

Days 3–5

Deep Research

• Add 1–2 more sources

• Organize notes: Early Life / The Passion / Challenges / Impact

• Quote 1–2 things the person actually said

Days 6–8

Create Product

• Choose: biography poster, children’s book, video, timeline, digital slideshow

• Draft and revise content

• Peer feedback session

Day 9–10

Present & Connect

• Present to class or small groups

• Personal connection: “This person inspires me because...”

• Reflection and portfolio capture

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Pre-selected list of inspirational people organized by interest category

•  Graphic organizer with 4 labeled sections

•  Sentence stems for personal connection paragraph

Extensions

•  Write a letter to the person (or their foundation)

•  Research a lesser-known figure in the same field

•  Create a short documentary instead of a poster

•  Compare two people with similar passions but different outcomes

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Research notes (4-section organizer)
  • Final product (photo or file)
  • Personal connection paragraph
  • Reflection

Project 5: The Big Question: A Science or Social Studies Investigation

⏱  Time Frame: 3–4 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What big question about the natural or social world am I curious about, and what can I find out?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Formulate a testable or researchable question connected to science or social studies content
  • Gather evidence from experiments, observations, or research
  • Organize findings into a structured report or display
  • Draw conclusions and propose next steps based on evidence

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Big Question Launch

• Wonder wall: students post questions they have about the world

• Sort questions: science / social studies / opinion / too small

• Choose one big, researchable question

Days 3–5

Plan Investigation

• Decide: Research project OR experiment?

• If research: choose 3+ sources

• If experiment: write a hypothesis and design the test

Days 6–10

Investigate

• Conduct research or experiment

• Record data or notes daily

• Mini-lessons: reading charts/graphs, taking organized notes, citing sources

Days 11–13

Share Findings

• Create a product: science fair board, research paper, slideshow, or documentary

• Peer review before final presentation

• Present to class, teacher, or panel

🤖 AI Integration

Student Prompts (Supervised)

•  “Ask AI: What are 5 important vocabulary words about [topic] that I should understand?”

•  “Ask AI: What are some things scientists already know about [topic]?” Then find a second source to confirm.

•  “Ask AI: What is a weakness in my conclusion? What else could I investigate?”

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Sentence frames for hypothesis: “I think ___ because ___.”

•  Pre-made data recording chart

•  Experiment option with provided materials

•  Buddy system for research

Extensions

•  Submit findings to a science fair or district competition

•  Interview an expert (local scientist, historian, etc.)

•  Compare results with a partner who chose a different question

•  Create a follow-up question and design a second investigation

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Research notes or experiment data
  • Final product (photo, file, or display)
  • Conclusion paragraph
  • AI log (if used)
  • Reflection

Project 6: Teach the School: Create an Informational Resource

⏱  Time Frame: 3 weeks

❓  Driving Question: What do I know that others should learn, and how can I design something to teach them?

🎯 Learning Goals

  • Identify an audience and design a product that meets their specific needs
  • Research and synthesize information from 3+ credible sources
  • Apply writing, design, and communication skills to a real-world purpose
  • Evaluate whether the product actually taught the audience effectively

📅 Daily Breakdown

Day(s)

Focus

Activities

Days 1–2

Topic & Audience

• Choose a topic they know well or want to teach

• Define audience: kindergarteners, parents, teachers, other 4th graders

• Ask: What does my audience already know? What will surprise them?

Days 3–5

Research & Outline

• Find 3 sources; take structured notes

• Create an outline: 3–5 key points to teach

• Choose format: how-to guide, infographic, video, brochure, website page

Days 6–9

Create

• Build the product with audience in mind

• Check: Is this too hard for my audience? Too easy?

• Peer test: Have a classmate try to learn from it

Days 10–12

Publish & Measure Impact

• Share with actual audience (another class, school display, website)

• Gather 3 pieces of feedback from audience members

• Reflect on whether it worked

♿ Differentiation

Scaffolds

•  Outline template with 5 boxes

•  Sentence stems for how-to writing

•  Partner creates the product together

•  Limited scope: teach ONE specific fact really well

Extensions

•  Create two versions for different audiences

•  Translate into a second language (or create a bilingual version)

•  Present at a school board or community event

•  Write a reflection on what they would change after seeing audience reaction

📂 Portfolio Artifacts

  • Research notes + outline
  • Final product (file or photo)
  • Audience feedback (3+ responses)
  • Reflection: Did it work? How do you know?
